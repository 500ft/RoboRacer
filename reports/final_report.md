## Executive Review

### What was decided

The project has two connected evidence chains:

```text
Gym experiment
-> timestamped CSV telemetry
-> numerical/model validation
-> held-out identification
-> controller, estimator, and robustness decisions

Committed simulation envelope + locked sensor mass
-> hand calculation
-> 174.7 Hz modal failure against a 200 Hz guard
-> shorter/larger mast redesign
-> 330.1 Hz hand prediction
-> 285.5 Hz converged FEA result
-> physical compliance gate (registered; measurement pending)
```

The baseline 120 mm-long, 16 mm-OD, 1.5 mm-wall 6061-T6 tube passed the
strength check but failed its predeclared frequency guard: **174.7 Hz < 200
Hz**. A sweep selected a 100 mm-long, 20 mm-OD tube with the same material and
wall. The revised hand model predicted **330.1 Hz** and the higher-fidelity FEA
predicted **285.5 Hz**, which clears the guard by 1.43x. The final mesh change
was **-0.15% gauge stress, +0.30% tip deflection, and -1.42% first
frequency**, all inside the 5% convergence criterion.

Static FEA also agreed with the hand model inside the frozen +/-15% credibility
band: **17.4 MPa versus 17.1 MPa** at the mid-span gauge (+1.3%),
**0.176 mm versus 0.166 mm** tip deflection (+5.9%), and **285.5 Hz versus
330.1 Hz** first frequency (-13.5%). These are model-to-model checks. The
registered physical campaign will judge measured compliance separately under
the same +/-15% rule.

### Why the failure matters

The mast story is not "FEA confirms a chosen shape." The analysis rejected the
first design despite ample yield margin, identified stiffness as the binding
constraint, and spent two direct design levers - length and diameter - to fix
it. That decision cost only 1.4 g of mast mass while improving the crash-case
yield safety factor from 4.05 to 8.05.

## 1. Scope, Motivation, and Evidence Labels

The practical motivation is to build credible RoboRacer engineering evidence
before access to a complete physical vehicle. The simulation lane establishes
repeatable modeling, identification, control, estimation, and failure-analysis
methods. The parallel design lane specifies a restricted-class hardware
platform and applies those simulation-derived loads to a buildable mechanical
decision. This is the simulation/control and design-review half of the
portfolio; future hardware data must earn its own claims.

## 3. Modeling Pipeline and Numerical Credibility

The simulator pipeline uses one importable `gym/roboracer/` code path for
numerics, dynamics, telemetry I/O, tracks, pure pursuit, estimation helpers,
and closed-loop runs. That reduces the risk that separate scripts silently use
different equations or conventions.

### Timestep selection

The RK4 sweep declared its criterion before interpreting results:

- RMS progress-aligned change between refinements < 0.05 m;
- maximum progress-aligned change < 0.2 m.

The selected **dt = 0.0020 s** is the coarsest tested timestep that clears both:
the next-finer comparison gives **0.0133307 m RMS** and **0.176077 m maximum**
progress-aligned change. The original 0.01 s baseline misses the maximum-change
criterion at 0.277026 m. Source:
[`reports/integrator_convergence.md`](integrator_convergence.md).

![Figure 1. RK4 position-error convergence across the tested timestep
refinements.](figures/integrator_convergence_position_error.png)

### Kinematic-to-dynamic boundary

The kinematic replay accumulates 66.7991 m position RMSE across the full
dynamic lap, while its instantaneous yaw law matches Gym at low speed with a
median ratio of 0.999. The combination rules out a simple sign, wheelbase, or
timestep bug and points to the expected missing lateral/yaw dynamics. Figure 2
shows the resulting trajectory error. Source:
[`reports/model_vs_gym_comparison.md`](model_vs_gym_comparison.md).

![Figure 2. Kinematic replay versus Gym trajectory; the growing error motivates

## 4. Identification and Held-Out Validation

The nonlinear single-track coefficients `C_Sf` and `C_Sr` are fit with bounded
nonlinear least squares on normalized one-step yaw-rate and slip-angle
residuals. Intervals below 0.75 m/s are excluded because Gym uses a low-speed
kinematic fallback there. The first 70% of eligible intervals form the fit set;
the final 30% are held out chronologically. The held-out rollout begins from
one measured split state and then propagates recursively without state resets.

| Parameter | Identified | Gym oracle | Relative error |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `C_Sf` | 4.717999998 | 4.718000000 | 5.197e-10 |
| `C_Sr` | 5.456200004 | 5.456200000 | 6.527e-10 |

The independent held-out replay passes all declared gates:

- yaw-rate VAF 100.000%, RMSE 3.028881e-10 rad/s;
- slip-angle VAF 100.000%, RMSE 2.877898e-10 rad;
- yaw RMSE 3.846e-10 rad;
- position RMSE 2.067e-09 m;
- normalized-residual Jacobian condition number 1.340140; and
- `C_Sf`/`C_Sr` parameter correlation 0.090721.

Source:
[`reports/dynamic_parameter_identification.md`](dynamic_parameter_identification.md)
and `runs/parameter_id_robustness/metrics.csv`.

### Interpretation

This is an oracle-recovery check: clean data was generated by the same simulator
model being fitted. The result proves that the pipeline, parameterization, and
held-out gate are internally coherent. It does not prove that these coefficients
describe a physical car. Section 6 deliberately degrades the data to show where
that credibility fails.

## 5. Controller and Estimator Decisions

Pure pursuit, LQR, and constrained MPC use the same map, RK4 `dt = 0.002 s`,
and 100 Hz zero-order-held command. LQR and MPC apply bounded corrections on
top of the selected pure-pursuit feedforward so the comparison asks whether
model-based correction improves the same baseline.

| Controller | Lap time (s) | RMS CTE (m) | Max CTE (m) | Steering effort (rad) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Pure pursuit | 38.042 | **0.157892** | 0.403062 | **8.19853** |
| LQR | 38.064 | 0.178628 | 0.497708 | 8.98777 |
| MPC | 37.860 | 0.169238 | 0.476650 | 8.67710 |

**Decision:** pure pursuit remains the tracking baseline because it has the
lowest RMS CTE and steering effort. LQR is stable and survives the defined
offset and delay cases, but does not improve nominal tracking. MPC earns its
place through explicit steering/rate/acceleration constraints and a p95 solve
time of **1.32644 ms**, not lower CTE. Its 36.807 ms maximum solve-time spike
exceeds one 10 ms control period and remains a deployment risk.

Controller sources: `runs/controller_comparison/results.csv` and
`runs/mpc_controller/results.csv`.

baseline.](figures/pure_pursuit_sweep_rms_cte_heatmap.png)

![Figure 4. MPC timing distribution against the real-time control

## 6. Failure Modes and Sim-to-Real Robustness

The FMEA reproduces or quantifies seven controller, estimator, numerical,
latency, and actuator cases. The highest RPNs are:

| Scenario | Detection signal | RPN | Decision |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| 100 ms latency | collision at 1.484 s | 200 | timestamp, reduce delay, compensate |
| high sensor noise | EKF RMSE 0.053 m | 180 | tune covariance, reject outliers |
| 3 s dropout | EKF RMSE 0.706 m | 168 | detect stale data, slow or stop |

High noise and the Euler case are quantified but not driven to a hard failure,
so their `reproduced` flags remain false. That distinction is preserved rather
than converted into a stronger claim.

### Identification robustness

The near-oracle parameter fit first fails acceptance at `noise_medium` on the
held-out yaw-rate gate. Latency is more damaging: 20 ms drives the `C_Sf`
relative error to approximately 0.440, and 50 ms drives it to approximately
0.892. The hardware identification plan therefore treats timestamp integrity
and end-to-end latency as first-order acceptance concerns.

## 8. Mechanical Design and Analysis

### Load path and assumptions

The mast is modeled as a fixed-root cantilever carrying a 0.175 kg tip
assembly. The mass consists of the 0.130 kg Hokuyo body plus assumed bracket,
fastener, and cable allowances. The clean selected simulation lap supplies
**19.436 m/s^2** peak lateral acceleration. A 50 g shock level with 1.5 safety
factor is analyzed separately and governs strength.

```text
LiDAR + bracket + cable, m_tip = 0.175 kg
             |
             v  lateral tip load
       [100 mm mast]
             |
     fixed deck interface
             |
        chassis / tires
```

The baseline hand calculation is committed at
`runs/mast_hand_calc/summary.txt`. It uses a 120 mm-long, 16 mm-OD, 1.5 mm-wall
6061-T6 tube. The maneuver load is 6.79 N. The 50 g shock case applies
**128.76 N**, producing 68.10 MPa root bending stress, 0.5931 mm tip
deflection, and yield safety factor 4.05. Strength passes.

### The binding failure

The same baseline produces **174.7 Hz** in the Rayleigh tip-mass model. The
criterion requires the first mode to clear the 100 Hz control rate and a
low-hundreds-Hz drivetrain band with a **200 Hz guard**. The baseline therefore
fails even though its strength margin is comfortable.

### Redesign

The committed sweep at `runs/mast_hand_calc/design_sweep.txt` evaluates length,
diameter, wall, and material. The selected configuration is:

| Attribute | Baseline | Revised |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Material | 6061-T6 | 6061-T6 |
| Length | 120 mm | 100 mm |
| OD | 16 mm | 20 mm |
| Wall | 1.5 mm | 1.5 mm |
| Hand `f1` | 174.7 Hz FAIL | 330.1 Hz PASS |
| Crash stress | 68.1 MPa | 34.3 MPa |
| Crash yield SF | 4.05 | 8.05 |
| Mast mass | 22.1 g | 23.5 g |

Shortening raises stiffness with the inverse cube of length; increasing OD
raises the section moment faster than it raises effective modal mass. Together
the changes raise stiffness 3.58x for a 1.4 g mass penalty while improving
strength.

## 9. Static FEA, Modal Validation, and Convergence

The revised idealized tube is meshed with 28,985 quadratic C3D10 tetrahedra
(58,232 nodes, target size 1.2 mm) and solved in CalculiX. The root is fixed.
The 128.8 N crash load is distributed over 474 tip-ring nodes to avoid a
nonphysical point-load singularity. Stress is compared on a fixed mid-span
gauge region; the global tip displacement provides the second static check.

### Hand-to-FEA agreement

| Quantity | Hand calc | FEA | Difference | +/-15%? |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| First bending frequency | 330.1 Hz | 285.5 Hz | -13.5% | PASS |
| Tip deflection | 0.166 mm | 0.176 mm | +5.9% | PASS |
| Mid-span gauge stress | 17.1 MPa | 17.4 MPa | +1.3% | PASS |

The 41.9 MPa global peak occurs at the fixed-root re-entrant corner. It is a
stress-concentration/mesh-singularity value and is not compared with elementary
beam theory. The fixed gauge region and global deflection are the reportable
static metrics.

### Modal decision

The first two FEA modes are the near-degenerate bending pair at **285.5 Hz**
and 301.3 Hz; the third is 1009.3 Hz. The higher-fidelity first mode remains
above the 200 Hz guard by 1.43x and above the 100 Hz control rate by 2.86x.
The roughly 14% reduction from the Rayleigh estimate is consistent with the
closed-form model's rigid-root and Euler-Bernoulli assumptions.

### Mesh convergence

| Mesh | Gauge stress | Tip deflection | `f1` |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 3.0 mm / 4,747 elements | 17.38 MPa | 0.1772 mm | 290.6 Hz |
| 2.0 mm / 10,435 elements | 17.50 MPa | 0.1751 mm | 289.6 Hz |
| 1.2 mm / 28,985 elements | 17.48 MPa | 0.1757 mm | 285.5 Hz |
| Final change | **-0.15%** | **0.30%** | **-1.42%** |

All final changes are below 1.5%, an order of magnitude inside the 5% gate.
Sources: `runs/mast_fea/fea_summary.txt` and
`runs/mast_fea/mesh_convergence.txt`.

## 10. Tolerances, Installation, and the Physical Compliance Gate

### Tolerance-driven design decision

The UST-10LX scans a horizontal plane, so the governing stack is pitch/roll
tilt rather than software-calibratable yaw. At a 0.120 m assumed deck height,
the optical center is 0.220 m above the floor. A 0.30 m wall at 10 m creates a
0.458 deg up-tilt bound.

The deterministic run at `runs/mast_tolerance_stack/summary.txt` reports:

| Condition | Tilt | Verdict |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Blind worst-case stack | **1.284 deg** | FAIL |
| Blind RSS stack | 0.652 deg | FAIL |
| After scan-plane leveling | **0.354 deg** | PASS, 1.29x margin |

The failure creates two requirements:

1. level the scan plane once at installation by shimming the mast base and
   equalizing wall return heights at two distances; and
2. keep the mast-root deck plane **<= 0.138 m** above the floor for the
   calibrated residual to clear the 0.30 m wall bound.

The tolerance inputs remain assumed until CAD and inspection replace them.
The conclusion is still useful: blind assembly is unacceptable, and the design
now has an install procedure plus a hard deck-height limit.

### Physical Compliance Gate

**Experiment registered - calibrated compliance campaign scheduled for July
25-August 2, 2026; measurement pending.**

The frozen protocol tests the 100 mm-long, 20 mm-OD, 1.5 mm-wall 6061-T6 mast
in both orthogonal axes:

- approximately 4, 8, 12, 16, and 20 N;
- at least three complete load/unload cycles at every level;
- 0.001 mm indicator resolution or better;
- fixture-motion subtraction on every row;
- force and displacement calibration; and
- saved raw rows `axis,cycle,direction,force_n,tip_mm,fixture_mm`.

The analyzer fits compliance with a free intercept. A result is INCONCLUSIVE if
either axis has R^2 < 0.99, hysteresis > 5% of full-scale displacement, missing
fixture motion, or relative expanded uncertainty U95 > 10%. If quality passes,
each measured as-built compliance must be within **+/-15%** of the as-built FEA
prediction for VALIDATED; otherwise the verdict is DISCREPANCY. Thresholds do
not move after data is visible.

The experiment measures compliance only. It **does not validate stress**,
fatigue life, on-car vibration, perception, or crash survival.

Source:
[`docs/specs/mast-physical-validation/design.md`](../docs/specs/mast-physical-validation/design.md).

## 11. Integration Risks, Limitations, and Next Gates

### Known limitations

- The controls and identification results are simulation-only and use one map.
- Oracle recovery is not physical parameter validation.
- MPC timing is implementation-specific; its maximum solve time exceeds one
  control period.
- FEA uses an idealized tube and fixed root; clamp, fastener, and as-built
  geometry are not yet represented.
- The 50 g shock, bracket/cable mass, and several tolerance contributors are
  explicit assumptions.
- The 4.42 kg mass is a bottom-up estimate, not a scale measurement.
- No on-car excitation spectrum exists, so separation from operational
  vibration remains unvalidated.
- No physical mast or vehicle result is claimed.

### Downstream gates

1. **CAD:** model the L=100 mm / OD=20 mm / t=1.5 mm mast and deck interface;
   enforce deck <= 0.138 m and the required front transponder bay.
2. **Fabrication:** issue a drawing/traveler, record stock certificate or seller
   specification, clamp/fastener condition, process, and lead time.
3. **Inspection:** record as-built length, OD, wall, clamp engagement, load
   height, and both test axes.
4. **As-built model:** regenerate hand and FEA compliance with those
   dimensions.
5. **Campaign:** execute the frozen matrix and publish the raw data,
   calibration, uncertainty, plot, and verdict.
6. **Hardware controls:** repeat excitation and held-out identification on the
   physical platform; do not transfer simulator coefficients.

The report is complete because each unresolved engineering activity has a
named input, owner-facing action, and acceptance gate. It is not completed by
silently converting pending evidence into a result.
